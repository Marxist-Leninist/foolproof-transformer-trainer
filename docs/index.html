Teach it your own words instead of Shakespeare

Paste anything you like: a story, song words you wrote, your own messages. It stays in this page on your device; this website has no server to send it to.

It needs at least 2,000 letters, about one page.

What to expect. This one is tiny, a few hundred thousand numbers at most, and it learns one letter at a time. In a minute or two it picks up spelling, spacing and the look of a play. It will not make sense: sense needs a far bigger network and far more reading, which is what the app below is for.

Now train a proper one on your own computer

The page above is the taster. The free app does the real thing: it uses your graphics card, reads whole books or any dataset on Hugging Face, trains for five minutes or all night, and lets you chat with what you made. It has this same block builder inside.

Windows 10 or 11

  1. Download the ZIP and open it (double-click it in your Downloads folder).
  2. Inside, double-click START-WINDOWS.bat. If Windows asks whether to Extract all or Run, choose Run.
  3. Wait while the black window sets things up. Your browser then opens by itself.

If a blue box says “Windows protected your PC”, click More info, then Run anyway. Windows shows that for every program whose maker has not bought a certificate from Microsoft.

Mac or Linux

  1. Open the Terminal app. On a Mac: press Cmd and Space, type Terminal, press Enter.
  2. Copy this line, paste it into the Terminal window and press Enter: curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Marxist-Leninist/foolproof-transformer-trainer/main/start.sh | bash
  3. Wait while it sets things up. Your browser then opens by itself.

Prefer to look first? Read the script, or download the ZIP and run start.sh from it.

The first start needs the internet and downloads Python and PyTorch: a few minutes and about 2 GB of disk, or 5 GB with an NVIDIA graphics card. After that it starts in seconds and works offline. Every change to the app is started for real on Windows, macOS and Linux machines before it counts as working: see the latest results. Graphics-card training itself has not been checked on every kind of card.

Good questions

Is the network on this page real?

Yes. It is a transformer: an embedding table, your blocks in your order, and an output layer, with every number in it adjusted by gradient descent while you watch. It is written out by hand in JavaScript, with the heavy multiplying done in WebAssembly, and it runs on your own processor. It does the same sums as the app’s PyTorch model: an automatic test loads the same numbers into both and checks that the answers and every gradient agree to about one part in a million. The tests are here.

Will this make something like ChatGPT?

No. It trains small networks from nothing, for learning and for fun. A short run on a laptop picks up the style of its text and nothing more; it may write nonsense, and what it writes is not fact. The well-known chatbots are around 100,000 times bigger and have read a large part of the internet.

Why does it write rubbish at first?

Because it starts with random numbers. Each step it guesses the next letter, sees the right answer, and nudges its numbers a little. First it learns which letters are common, then where spaces go, then short words, then the shape of a line. The five-step ladder shows how far it has got.

What do the blocks do?

Attention lets it look back over the letters it has already read and pick out the ones that matter. Feed-forward is where it works something out from what it found, and where most of what it learns is kept. A Transformer layer is one of each, the pair every modern language model stacks up. Try it without Attention and watch it struggle: it can then only see one letter at a time.

Where does my text go?

Nowhere. On this page the learning happens in your browser and the page has no server to send anything to. The app also trains on your own computer; it only goes online to fetch what you ask for, such as a dataset from Hugging Face, or to publish a model if you choose to.

What is a Hugging Face dataset?

A dataset is a big collection of text for an AI to learn from. Hugging Face is a website where people share thousands of them for free. The app has a search box for it and shows you a preview first. Each one has its own licence, so check that before you share what you make from it.

Where are the code and the full instructions?

On GitHub, with the MIT licence, the setup guide and the test results. It is free to use, change and share.
